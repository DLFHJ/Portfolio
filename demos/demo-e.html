Urban Interface

Category

← Back to Work

This project explores how public digital interfaces can reshape the way people move through and experience urban space. Through participatory research and iterative prototyping, a series of location-aware installations were developed that adapt to the rhythms of the city.

Early-stage research: field observations at three city intersections were used to map pedestrian flows and identify key moments of disorientation or hesitation.

The prototyping phase consisted of three rapid iteration cycles. Each cycle introduced physical and digital components — from pressure-sensitive flooring to ambient audio cues — tested directly in a simulated urban context. Participants navigated the environment while thinking aloud, providing rich qualitative data that directly shaped subsequent design decisions.

Final prototype installed during a two-week public pilot. Visitor interactions were logged and analyzed to measure usability and emotional response.

This project demonstrated that small, context-aware interventions can meaningfully shift how people perceive and interact with public space — a finding that directly informs future smart city design strategies.
